# How to read your rent statement and pay what you owe

Your rent statement shows how much you owe and the date to pay it by. Look at the top of
the statement: the figure marked **Balance due** is the amount to pay, and the line
marked **Payment due date** is the day we must receive it by. If the balance due is
£42.50 and the date is 1 July 2026, you need to pay £42.50 so that it reaches us on or
before 1 July 2026.

## What the words on your statement mean

Your statement uses a few words in a particular way:

- **arrears** — rent you owe from an earlier date that has not yet been paid, on top of
  this period's rent
- **a debit** — an amount added to what you owe, such as a week's rent
- **a credit** — an amount taken off what you owe, such as a payment you have made or
  Housing Benefit paid on your behalf

If your statement shows a balance in credit, you do not owe anything and you do not need
to do anything.

## How to pay

There are 3 ways to pay what you owe:

1. Pay online at the council website. You will need your rent account number, which is
   printed at the top of your statement.
2. Pay by telephone using a debit or credit card. Call the number shown on your
   statement and have your rent account number ready.
3. Set up a Direct Debit so that the rent is paid automatically each month. A Direct
   Debit is an instruction to your bank to pay us on a set date.

Pay the balance due so that it reaches us on or before the payment due date. If you
cannot pay the full amount by that date, contact the rents team before the date, because
we may be able to agree a payment arrangement with you.

## If the amount does not look right

If the balance due does not look right to you, do not ignore the statement. Contact the
rents team on the number printed on your statement, or reply to the letter that came
with it, and explain what you think is wrong. We will check your account and correct it
if there has been a mistake. Continue to pay any amount you agree is correct while the
query is being looked into.
